Smoke analysis

How smoke particles change as they age

NASA's SAME flight tests reported the arithmetic mean smoke particle diameter at baseline and after aging. All bars use the same 0 to 450 nm scale.

  • Baseline (no aging)
  • Aged 720 s

    0 150 300 450 nm

    The reported mean diameter was larger after aging for all four materials. Matching smoke age would make a follow-up detector comparison easier to interpret. The detector study describes the alarm thresholds separately.

    Related NASA research The Lunar Combustion Investigation measured how flames spread on cotton-fiberglass fabric and acrylic in simulated lunar gravity. Its measurements describe material flammability. See the LUCI flight results.

    Proposed lunar follow-up Compare the same smoke detector and selected material in microgravity and simulated lunar gravity. Match airflow, smoke aging time, oxygen concentration, and pressure. Record the detector signal and whether and when it alarms.

      Proposed test: Compare the ISS and Shuttle detector types with the same Teflon smoke exposure, airflow, and smoke aging time. That would help isolate how the detector type affects the response.
